    AI traceability for CNP: legal map (EU/France) and VictoriaStack implementation¶

    Research date: 2026-11-13. This is a factual map, not legal advice. Convention: "LAW SAYS" = binding text. "GUIDANCE" = non-binding official text. "BEST PRACTICE" = my synthesis or practitioner advice. Confidence: [HIGH] primary source read this session. [MED] secondary source or primary source only partly read. [LOW] inferred or unverified. Stale flag: anything older than 12 months (before 2025-11-13) is marked [STALE - date].

    Access caveat: eur-lex.europa.eu pages do not render through my fetcher (JS-only). For AI Act article text I used the European Commission "AI Act Service Desk" article pages, which state they follow the EUR-Lex consolidated version of 2026-07-27 and flag Omnibus changes with NEW/AMENDED markers. The EUR-Lex consolidated text exists at CELEX 02024R1689-20260727 (seen in search results, not rendered). Treat EUR-Lex as the binding source and re-check anything load-bearing there.

    PART A. LEGAL¶

    A1. EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689) as amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI¶

Primary text not read in full. Duties then: Art. 4 (support literacy), Art. 5 (prohibitions), Art. 50(1) disclosure unless obvious, possibly Art. 50(2) marking of generated text if the exemption for assistive editing does not fit. Whether Art. 50(2) bites on internal dev agents is an open reading.     A2. GDPR and CNIL¶

    LAW SAYS (GDPR, Reg. 2016/679; ELI https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj, not re-fetched this session, article numbers from standard knowledge) [MED] - Art. 5(1)© minimisation, 5(1)(e) storage limitation, 5(1)(f) integrity and confidentiality, 5(2) accountability, Art. 25 by design, Art. 32 security, Art. 30 register, Art. 35 DPIA, Art. 15 access, Art. 22 automated decisions, Art. 33-34 breach notification (72 h to supervisory authority). - Prompts and agent traces routinely contain personal data (names, emails, code with data, tickets). So prompt/response logs are personal data processing with CNP or the tenant as controller. A controller needs a purpose and a legal basis for the log processing itself.

    CNIL logging recommendation: deliberation no. 2021-122 of 2021-10-14 [HIGH, read in full] [STALE - 2021-10-14, still the reference] PDF: https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/recommandation_-_journalisation.pdf . Legifrance JORFTEXT000044272396. - Scope: application logs, not perimeter logs (para. 2). Non-prescriptive (para. 1). - Para. 6: record creation, consultation, modification, deletion with individually identified author, timestamp, nature of operation, reference of the data concerned. "Avoid duplicating the processed data within the logs." - Para. 7: store segregated from the main system, write-only for the applications, no overwrite; access rights to logs based on strict necessity. - Para. 8: retain 6 months to 1 year. Para. 9: purpose is mainly active security (alerts, detection), formalise alert processing. Para. 10: ex post breach investigation. - Para. 11: reuse for another purpose may be a purpose diversion; use a usage charter or alert on modification access. - Para. 12: minimise personal data in logs; ensure confidentiality, availability, integrity; recommends to timestamp and sign logs at creation. - Para. 13: inform authorised users of logging, data and duration. Data subject rights on logs are exercised with the controller of the main processing. - Paras. 16-17: more than one year can be justified for internal control if risk of misuse is demonstrated (sensitive data at scale, systematic surveillance) and documented investigation procedures exist. - Para. 18: do not collect sensitive or highly personal data in logs that is not already in the processing.

    CNIL AI recommendations [MED, STALE for 2024-2025 fiches] - Fiches IA (2024-04 to 2025-07) cover development of AI systems: legal basis, DPIA, security, rights, annotation, model status. No fiche on logs, none dedicated to deployment. List: https://www.cnil.fr/fr/les-fiches-pratiques-ia . [HIGH] [STALE - 2025-07] - Rights over prompts: CNIL states GDPR applies to use of models through prompts, and rights requests should say whether they target training data or the model. https://www.cnil.fr/fr/ia-respecter-lexercice-des-droits-des-personnes [MED, STALE] - EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (2024-12-17): models are not automatically anonymous, legitimate interest needs a three-step test. [MED, STALE - 2024-12-17] - CNIL/CIANum note "IA agentique et protection des donnees personnelles", July 2026 [HIGH, read]. https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-07/ia-cianum-cnil.pdf . This is an analysis note, not a formal CNIL deliberation, and says the AI Act (full application 2027) has no specific agent rules. It proposes: - Traceability so that for each task the user can identify personal data used, agents involved, third-party services called, exchanges and their chronology. This also builds a chain of responsibility. - Data minimisation and purpose limitation per task, user control over accessible data. - Art. 22 GDPR remains fully applicable. CJEU SCHUFA ruling: human intervention must be meaningful. - Memory partitioned per agent, expiry or size limits, one session per processing. - Sandboxing, human validation for critical actions classified by risk (data access, modification, deletion, outbound data), kill switch, filtering of inputs/outputs, evaluation. - Notes that joint EDPB/Commission guidelines on AI Act and GDPR interplay are expected by end 2026 (not found published). [LOW] - ANSSI "Les essentiels: Securiser un systeme IA agentique" v1.0, October 2026 [HIGH, read]. https://messervices.cyber.gouv.fr/documents-guides/anssi_essentiels_ia_agentique_1.0.pdf . Seven principles. Directly relevant items: - Classify agent actions as autonomous, mandatory human approval, or forbidden. Human approval for critical actions, sensitive data access, identity or rights changes, production deployments. Keep the approval mechanism out of the agent's reach. Show the approver the exact action, purpose, expected effects. Execute only the approved action. - Deterministic authorisation independent of the agent, default deny. - Keep secrets out of model context, memory and logs. - Keep agent logs out of reach of agent modification, so the full sequence "request, authorisation, action, result" can be reconstructed after an incident. - Monitor in production against the documented mission (UEBA), emergency stop with access revocation, per-tenant and per-environment memory. - Cites the AI Act Art. 6 for high-risk systems. - ANSSI "Recommandations de securite pour un systeme d'IA generative" ANSSI-PA-102, 2024-04-29 [STALE - 2024-04-29]: R9, no automatic execution of critical IS actions. [MED, from search snippet]

    Tension "log everything" vs "minimise" and how it resolves [BEST PRACTICE] - The AI Act has no logging duty for CNP today, and for high-risk the 6-month floor in Art. 19/26(6) is explicitly subject to data protection law. So the legal floor for content logging is zero for CNP, and the GDPR sets a ceiling by purpose. - Pattern: always log metadata and decisions (who, what, when, model, tokens, tool, approval, outcome, hashes), never content by default, content capture opt-in per project with a stated purpose, short retention for content, redaction at the gateway or collector, hash of content so integrity and non-repudiation hold without the text. - This matches the OTel spec default (content capture off) and CNIL para. 6, 12, 18.

    Tamper-evident audit trail options for a small stack [BEST PRACTICE, tools unevaluated] 1. S3 Object Lock in compliance mode (you already have it) as the system of record for the per-run "AI Act record", written at run end as a signed JSON object. This is WORM by storage guarantee. Matches CNIL para. 7 (write-only, segregated) and ANSSI principle 7 (outside agent reach). 2. Hash chaining: each run record includes prev_hash and its own SHA-256; write the daily chain head into Object Lock. Cheap, detects deletion and edit. Matches CNIL para. 12 "timestamp and sign at creation". 3. Signing: sign the daily manifest with a key held in Vault transit (you run Vault) or cosign. Key never visible to agents. 4. Transparency log: Sigstore Rekor v2 is tile-based, needs rekor-tiles plus a timestamp authority, no online inclusion proof API, so it is heavy for a small team. Single-binary Merkle logs such as Etchlog (RFC 6962, single-node) exist, maturity unchecked. Source: Sigstore docs and project READMEs via search. [LOW]. I would skip this for the demo. 5. VictoriaLogs and VictoriaTraces are mutable operational stores. Do not present them as the evidence store. Present them as the query layer, with the Object Lock copy as evidence.

    B5. What "impressive and compliant" looks like for the demo¶

    One run, one trace, one record: 1. Root span at the CMP/MCP entry: Keycloak user (sub, hashed email), project/tenant, assistant client, requested action, correlation id. Disclosure shown to the user (Art. 50(1) evidence). 2. Child spans: invoke_agent (kagent), chat spans per model call through agentgateway (provider, model, tokens, cost, guardrail verdicts), execute_tool spans per MCP call (tool name, argument hash), sandbox exec span (command hash, exit code, egress policy hits), then the deployment span (Argo CD sync, commit SHA, image digest). 3. Approval events in the same trace: needs_user_confirmation asked, answered by whom, needs_cmp_approval pending, approved by whom in CMP, the exact action hash executed. The Kubernetes, Vault, Keycloak and Argo CD audit logs already exist and join on user and time. Link them by commit SHA, deployment name and Keycloak sub. 4. Grafana "run view" dashboard: trace waterfall, cost, approval timeline, guardrail hits, link to the evidence object. 5. "AI Act record" export per run: a signed JSON (and optional PDF) produced by a small Go tool querying VictoriaTraces and VictoriaLogs by trace_id. Fields: run id, time window, user, project, system and model versions (agent version, gateway version, model name and provider, local model digest), disclosure version, list of model calls with token and cost, list of tool calls with argument hashes, approvals with approver identity, guardrail verdicts, resulting artefacts (commit, image digest, deployment), content references (pointer to opt-in content object or "not captured"), prev_hash and hash. Stored in the Object Lock bucket. Same fields map to Art. 12(2) purposes and the CNIL "demande, autorisation, action, resultat" sequence of ANSSI. 6. Privacy posture to show: content capture off by default, per-project opt-in switch, redaction at the gateway, retention dashboard, a DSAR search by hashed user id. 7. Label it honestly: "built to the Art. 12/19/26(6) shape, not a conformity claim, no harmonised standard yet".
